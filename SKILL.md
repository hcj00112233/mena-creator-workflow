# MENA Creator Workflow

Support creator partnerships with clear role-aware planning and careful script or brief work. Communicate in the user's language by default; write creator-facing content in the brief's requested language and market voice. This workflow is portable plain text. Do not assume access to a platform, tool, account, or external document.

## Orient before drafting

Extract what is available from the request and supplied materials: (1) collaboration role, (2) client industry, (3) target market and language, (4) campaign goal, (5) responsibilities and approval owners, (6) requested deliverables, and (7) brief/version status. Do not treat an attached client brief as permission to contact people, edit a live source, publish, or approve on anyone's behalf.

Use the user's stated goal and brief as the primary direction. Separate role from industry: the role says what work and decisions to emphasize; the industry informs likely content patterns. Read [references/role-industry.md](references/role-industry.md) when these distinctions affect scope. If a missing detail could change factual claims, rights, safety, market/language, or who approves, ask up to three concise questions together. Otherwise state a small set of assumptions and proceed. Never imply the creator or client has approved unresolved work.

## Choose the work shape

- For a creator script, script review, or revision, use [references/script-review.md](references/script-review.md). Preserve the original idea where possible and connect each material note to a brief requirement, evidence, shot, or line.
- For Saudi Arabic copy or a Saudi creator script, also use [references/saudi-arabic-review.md](references/saudi-arabic-review.md). Treat it as a review aid, not a guarantee of native voice. For other markets, do not impose Saudi dialect.
- For a short creator brief, use the relevant sections in [assets/output-templates.md](assets/output-templates.md). Keep only fields useful for the assignment.
- For GTM or campaign strategy, offer a light structure around positioning, route to market, channel choices, and testable experiments. Do not present this skill as comprehensive specialist strategy.

## Deliver clearly

Make the output easy to act on: distinguish confirmed facts, client-provided claims, assumptions, and open questions. Use severity labels only when they help prioritize. Explain the consequence of a concern and offer an edit or option when possible. Do not invent a performance score, forecast, or promised lift. Separate a recommendation from a required client decision.

When reviewing a draft, distinguish objective errors or brief conflicts from taste preferences. Preserve creator voice and intent where the brief allows. Quote the relevant original line or shot and provide a replacement only when the change is supported. For disputes between client requirements and creator willingness, lay out workable options and mark the decision as pending with the responsible parties.
