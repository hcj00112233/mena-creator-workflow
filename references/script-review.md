# Creator script review

Use this for a draft, storyboard, or a requested rewrite. Keep notes tied to evidence and the actual brief. A compact response may use: priority, location/original, issue and reason, suggested edit, and decision owner.

## Review sequence

1. Read the brief and supplied source material. Record the requested audience, goal, must-have messages, prohibited claims, deliverables, runtime, language, creator constraints, and approval route that are actually stated.
2. Resolve version status. If files conflict, identify the conflicting versions and ask which is current. Never assume the newest filename or timestamp is approved.
3. Map each material brief requirement to a specific shot, spoken line, on-screen text, or CTA. Mark absent support as missing; do not claim a visual or spoken proof that is not in the draft.
4. Check claims with provenance. Separate brand/product facts backed by supplied product material from client-provided assertions. A client assertion is not independent verification. Do not manufacture personal use, results, comparisons, testimonials, or test conditions.
5. Check timing and production feasibility. Add timed sections to compare estimated runtime with stated runtime; distinguish a runtime range from a nominal duration. Flag conflicting timing, missing transitions, impractical shots, unclear footage or graphics, and edit-dependent claims.
6. Check hook, audience relevance, CTA-to-goal fit, creator style, and market language. Treat taste as preference unless it conflicts with a stated requirement or demonstrable audience need.
7. Separate decisions that belong to the brand, creator, or agency. Give alternatives where requirements conflict; leave unresolved rights, competitor exposure, or creative consent pending with the right owners.
8. Return concise prioritized notes. Preserve the central idea unless it blocks a brief requirement, truthfulness, or feasibility. Include a revised line or shot when a supported fix is clear.

## Camera and comparison checks

For camera, image, or performance demonstrations, separate the camera used to film the creator's B-roll or opening from the sample footage used as evidence. A selfie-camera opening does not invalidate a later rear-camera comparison by itself. Flag a camera mismatch only when samples from different cameras are directly presented as a like-for-like performance comparison. Then consider lens, processing, framing, distance, exposure, and stabilization; recommend controlled conditions when the claim depends on them: same light, subject, similar angle/distance, and equivalent displayed magnification where relevant. Do not compare a still photo's pixel dimensions to video resolution as if they were the same measure. State what is visible and what remains unproven. Do not write a result as already achieved if the footage has not been captured or reviewed.

## Language parity

When the deliverable includes Chinese and Arabic (or another pair), check that both versions preserve the same factual claims, offer, conditions, caveats, CTA, and level of certainty. Natural phrasing may differ; do not force word-for-word translation. Mark any meaning shift for resolution.
