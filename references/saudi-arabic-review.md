# Saudi Arabic review aid

This module is adapted from the user's installed `saudi-native-arabic` skill. It is a practical checklist, not a claim that a model or reviewer is a native speaker. Ask for a creator's approved samples or review by a fluent Saudi speaker when voice authenticity matters. Do not infer authorship from tone.

## First identify text type and market

Separate spoken lines, on-screen captions, social post copy, direct messages, technical names, and internal brief notes. Apply the requested format and platform conventions to each. This guidance is for Saudi contexts only; use the target market's own register elsewhere. Do not mechanically ban colons, dashes, numbering, Modern Standard Arabic, questions, or CTA forms across all text types.

## Preserve natural spoken structure

- Prefer a clear opening and short, speakable phrases. Read aloud for breath, rhythm, and transitions.
- Avoid translating English syntax literally. Rebuild the sentence in a natural order while preserving meaning.
- Use colloquial markers only when they fit this creator and context. Do not sprinkle `وايد`, `صج`, `أبدًا`, or other regional words as automatic decoration.
- Reduce filler, repeated claims, over-polished symmetry, and exaggerated praise. Keep a direct CTA if the brief requires one.
- Keep product and model names in Latin script where that helps recognition or search. Preserve technical terms consistently and explain only when the audience needs it.
- Keep numbers and punctuation in the requested house style. For mixed RTL/LTR strings, check that model names, units, URLs, and comparison symbols remain unambiguous.

## Review accuracy separately from voice

Label a proposed edit as one of: meaning/fact issue, brief compliance, readability, dialect/voice preference, or open question. A word substitution list is a prompt for judgment, never an automatic find-and-replace dictionary. Do not insert first-person experience such as “جربته” unless the creator actually supplied that experience. Do not promise that copy is fully native or ready to publish without the required human review.
