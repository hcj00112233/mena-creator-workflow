# Synthetic camera-script review example

This short example is invented for training. “Phone A” and “Phone B” are placeholders. It does not represent a real client, creator, specification, approval, or performance result.

## Brief fragment

- Requested video runtime: 35–45 seconds.
- Show a camera difference with a credible demonstration.
- Client asks for Phone B to appear in the comparison.
- Creator says they are not comfortable featuring a competitor device.
- No comparative footage has been recorded yet.

## Draft fragment

- 0:00–0:08: Creator speaks to a selfie camera and says Phone A is “better in low light.”
- 0:08–0:28: Planned split-screen comparison with Phone B, filmed later on the rear camera.
- 0:28–0:50: Creator gives the CTA.
- Script header says “40 seconds.”

## Review

**High — Runtime conflict.** The timed segments total 50 seconds, while the requested range ends at 45 seconds. Trim or retime the planned beats, then recalculate the full cut including transitions and any end card.

**High — Claim is not yet demonstrated.** “Better in low light” is a comparative performance claim, but no footage exists and the draft does not state matched conditions. Either record a fair test under the same light with the same subject, similar framing and distance, and comparable displayed magnification, or replace the line with a non-comparative, supportable description. Do not describe the test as passed before reviewing footage.

The selfie camera is used only to film the creator's opening, not as one of the comparison samples, so that shot alone does not create a camera-comparison issue. If selfie footage is later used as one of the samples, clarify that and review whether the claim still compares like with like.

**Pending — Competitor appearance and creator consent.** The client requested Phone B and the creator declined. The client and creator must resolve this. Options to discuss: (a) no competitor device, with Phone A shown in a standalone low-light demo; (b) a comparison using client-supplied footage whose source and usage rights are confirmed; or (c) creator records the comparison only if they agree to the device and conditions. None is approved by this example.

**Open fact check — Product claim source.** Confirm any low-light capability statement against current product material. Client-provided wording alone does not independently verify the claim.

**CTA — Goal fit.** Check that the CTA names the actual destination or offer and includes material conditions. The example brief fragment does not specify those details, so leave them open rather than inventing a link or discount.
